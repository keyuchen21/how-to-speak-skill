# Day-of checklist

Run through this before speaking. Every item traces to a rule in `rules.md`.

## Booking the slot (do this weeks early)

- [ ] **11 a.m. if there is a choice.** People are awake, nobody is going back to sleep,
      it is not right after a meal.
- [ ] **Room sized so it will be more than half full.** Ten people in a large hall makes
      the whole room wonder what went wrong. A packed room is not required; an empty-
      looking one is fatal.
- [ ] **Reject a dark room.** A room designed for watching movies is not a room for
      giving talks.

## Casing the place (on arrival, before the talk)

- [ ] Ask the host to walk to the actual room. Do not accept a description of it.
- [ ] Stand where the speaking happens. Find the weirdness now, so it is not a surprise
      later.
- [ ] Tell the audiovisual people: **keep the lights full up.** When they answer that the
      slides look better dark: *"it's extremely hard to see slides through closed
      eyelids."*
- [ ] Check the distance between the speaking position and the screen. Standing far from
      the screen makes the audience swing their heads like a tennis crowd.
- [ ] Find the board and check there is chalk or a working marker, if any part of the
      talk is taught rather than exposed.
- [ ] Put the props where they can be reached without breaking stride.
- [ ] Before starting, imagine every seat filled with **disinterested farm animals**.
      Nothing real can then be worse than what was imagined.

## Equipment

- [ ] **No laser pointer.** Leave it on the table. Every image that needs pointing at
      already has a numbered arrow drawn into it.
- [ ] Slide fonts at 40–50 pt, nothing under 35 pt.
- [ ] Deck printed once and laid out on a table, to check for air and imagery.
- [ ] Announce the rule of engagement if it is a teaching session: **no laptops, no
      phones.** One language processor per human; an open laptop distracts its neighbours
      and degrades the speaker.

## Body

- [ ] **Hands out of pockets.** Read as concealing something, and insulting in some
      cultures.
- [ ] **Hands not clasped behind the back.** Same reading, stronger insult.
- [ ] Give the hands a job: the board, the chalk, a prop. Pointing at the board works
      even when what is pointed at is unrelated to what is being said.
- [ ] Eyes on the room, never on the screen.

## Rehearsal (days before, not hours)

- [ ] Rehearse with friends who **do not know the work**. People who know it hallucinate
      material that is not in the talk.
- [ ] Do **not** rehearse with the supervisor or officemates for the same reason.
- [ ] Open the rehearsal with: *"if you can't make me cry, I won't value you as a friend
      any more."*
- [ ] Time the first five minutes separately. Vision and evidence of having done
      something must both be delivered inside them.
- [ ] Practise waiting **7 seconds** after the planned question, in silence, with a
      clock. It feels like an eternity from the front of the room.

## The opening 60 seconds

- [ ] Empowerment promise, spoken. What will they know at the end that they do not know
      now?
- [ ] Collaborators named here, on the first slide.
- [ ] No joke.

## The last 60 seconds

- [ ] Final slide reads **Contributions**. Not Conclusions, not Questions, not The End,
      not a URL, not a collaborator list.
- [ ] Final words chosen and rehearsed: a joke, a benediction, or a salute to the
      audience.
- [ ] **Do not say "thank you"** as the last words. Mouth it after the applause starts if
      the impulse is irresistible.

## Expectation setting for questions

- [ ] Expect the hardest questions from the youngest people present. Flak is inversely
      proportional to age.
- [ ] The Contributions slide is on screen throughout. It is the last thing the room
      reads, so it must say what was done.
